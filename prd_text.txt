Campus Placement Portal
Product Requirements Document (PRD) — Version 1.1
Revision note: Section 5 and 6 updated to rebalance workload between Shashwat and Arnav. See Section 5.1 for rationale.
1. Problem Statement
Campus recruitment at most institutions is still run through scattered spreadsheets and email threads maintained by the Training & Placement (T&P) cell. This causes duplicated and inconsistent student/company records, no single source of truth for where an application stands, manual and error-prone eligibility checks against CGPA/department/skill criteria, and poor visibility for students into their own application pipeline. The Campus Placement Portal replaces this with a single normalized relational database enforcing referential integrity, status consistency, and eligibility rules across every student, recruiter, and staff interaction.
2. Goals & Success Metrics

Metric

3. Users & Personas
Student — builds a profile, uploads a resume, lists skills, browses postings, applies, and tracks status through to offer.
Recruiter — registers a company, posts jobs with eligibility/package details, reviews applicants, schedules interviews, extends offers.
T&P Staff (Admin) — verifies applications, sends notifications, oversees the pipeline across departments and companies, views placement statistics.

5. Task & Role Division
All database design, schema, and query logic is primarily owned by Shashwat, and all UI/UX and role-based screens are primarily owned by Arnav. The four most design-intensive and time-consuming tasks in the project — schema design, eligibility logic, status-transition procedures, and the graph-DB extension — are shared jointly so that neither person absorbs a disproportionate share of the hardest work, and so Arnav's project experience and design input feed directly into these decisions rather than being confined to the UI layer.
5.1 Rebalance Rationale (v1.1)
The original split (13 tasks to Shashwat, 9 to Arnav) counted tasks but not effort or difficulty. Four backend tasks — schema design (BCNF), eligibility-matching logic, PL-SQL status-transition procedures, and the graph-DB skill-matching module — are substantially heavier and more open-ended than the remaining backend tasks (which are largely CRUD/registration/logging work following an established pattern). This revision moves those four heavy tasks to shared ownership, keeps the rest of the backend with Shashwat, and keeps all UI/UX with Arnav. The result is 9 solo tasks each, plus 6 shared tasks (the 4 heavy items plus the pre-existing shared integration testing and documentation).

6. Team Roles
Shashwat — Database & Backend Lead
Owns CRUD/registration/job-posting backend, application-submission logic, interview and offer backend logic, staff verification workflow, notification triggers, and placement-statistics queries. Jointly owns, with Arnav, the four heaviest design decisions: schema normalization (BCNF), eligibility-matching logic, PL-SQL status-transition procedures/triggers, and the skill-matching graph-DB extension.
Arnav — Frontend & UX Lead
Owns all role-based UI (Student, Recruiter, Staff), resume/skill input flows, application tracker UI, interview/offer UI, staff dashboard, notification inbox, recommendation UI, and the placement statistics dashboard. Jointly owns, with Shashwat, the four heaviest design decisions listed above, contributing design and UX judgment to those backend/data-model choices rather than being limited to the UI layer.
Integration Contract
For the tasks Shashwat owns solo, he exposes a documented data-access layer (functions/views with fixed inputs and outputs) that Arnav's frontend consumes, so each person can build against a fixed contract rather than a moving target. For the four shared tasks, both contribute directly to design and implementation rather than working through a handoff contract.

8. Risks
Single owner for most database/backend logic (Shashwat) remains a bottleneck for his solo tasks — mitigated by freezing the schema and data-access-layer contract early, and by sharing the four heaviest design tasks jointly so Arnav isn't purely a downstream consumer of backend decisions.
Frontend-backend contract mismatch — mitigated by documenting exact function signatures and sample outputs before UI work starts on each module.
Two-person bandwidth across a multi-module scope — P2 tasks (recommendation module, statistics dashboard) are explicitly cuttable/deferrable if P0/P1 work runs long.
Graph-DB extension may not clearly outperform the relational baseline for the chosen use case — mitigated by picking a use case (skill/job matching) where the graph model has a genuine structural advantage, and documenting trade-offs honestly.
No production-grade authentication — login/roles are simulated for MVP; explicitly acknowledged as an out-of-scope limitation.
Notifications are in-app only — no real email/SMS delivery in MVP; named as a known limitation rather than a hidden gap.
